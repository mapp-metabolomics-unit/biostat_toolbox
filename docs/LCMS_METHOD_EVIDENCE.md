# LC–MS method evidence for batch 00280

**Scope and strength of evidence.** Sun & Xia (2023) is a *review* of preprocessing options, not a head-to-head validation on these plants; Du (2014) is a teaching workshop, not a prospective benchmark. Both emphasize that the biological question, measurement process and model determine the choice. Neither establishes one universally best pipeline or validates this batch's numerical cutoffs. Sources: [Sun & Xia, abstract and “Preprocessing and pretreatment”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10827599/); [Du, pp. 4–7](https://www.uab.edu/proteomics/metabolomics/workshop/2014/statistical%20analysis.pdf). This audit covers the already-picked MZmine **height** matrix, not raw chromatogram peak detection/alignment; those upstream operations cannot be repaired by changing a toolbox normalization ([Sun & Xia, “MS-based data preprocessing”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10827599/); [`recipes/dataset.yaml`](../../laure-weisskopf-group/docs/mapp_project_00016/mapp_batch_00280/recipes/dataset.yaml)).

## Baseline audit and resulting implementation

Before the revision, [`study.yaml`](../../laure-weisskopf-group/docs/mapp_project_00016/mapp_batch_00280/recipes/study.yaml) used a sample/blank median ratio ≥5 when blank prevalence was ≥0.5, QC RSD ≤30%, **one global** half-minimum for all absent intensities, PQN, log2 and Pareto scaling. The previous [`preprocess.R`](../src/biostats/preprocess.R) imputed zeros/NA even in pooled QCs before estimating their RSD, letting undetected features appear perfectly stable. The *historical* sealed run retained 4,167 features (345 biological injections, 40 QCs, 9 blanks). Those observations explain the audit counts below; they are **not** counts from the newly corrected analysis. The revised toolbox retains the specified 5:1 blank rule, PQN, log2, Pareto and the 30% QC-RSD threshold, but excludes never-observed biological features, uses **per-feature biological** half-minima ([Sun & Xia, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10827599/)), and requires ≥80% observed-positive QC support (and ≥3 QCs) before observed-only normalized RSD can pass. It saves raw feature detection, blank support, QC support and acquisition-order QC diagnostics; QC LOESS correction is optional and **off** in these batch recipes. This corrects a measurement-support defect without claiming that any particular cutoff is universally optimal. [`analysis.R`](../src/biostats/analysis.R) uses scaled values for PCA, normalized nonnegative values for Bray–Curtis PCoA and log2-transformed (not Pareto-scaled) values for feature inference.

| Priority / status | Evidence → batch consequence and recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| **1 · Established principle; measure before changing.** Separate genuine absences, peak-picking failures and left-censoring; inspect observed detection by feature, biological group, batch and QC **before imputation**. | [Sun & Xia, “The sources of zeros”, “The approaches of dealing with zeros”, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10827599/) explicitly distinguish structural/sampling/below-detection zeros, say there is no agreed optimal imputation, and describe *per-feature* half-minimum. In the existing retained set, **142 features have zero observed positive intensity in every biological sample and 1,980 are detected in <20%** (audit of saved batch inputs). Global half-minimum makes those 142 constant-positive before log/PQN and can distort low-abundance comparisons, even if downstream variance masking later prevents some tests. Prioritize a pre-imputation detection-count/coverage report and sensitivity to excluding biologically all-absent features; compare feature-specific half-minimum only as an **optional sensitivity** where the feature has measured positives. An all-absent feature has no measured feature-specific minimum: do not invent one. Do **not** blindly apply a universal 80% threshold: Sun & Xia also discuss class-specific detection and the risk of discarding true group-specific signals; thresholds must be declared before inferential testing and evaluated for selection bias. |
| **2 · Established QC principle; observed-support correction before tuning cutoffs.** Keep role-aware blanks and pooled QCs; inspect raw/normalized QC detection and CV, blank intensity/prevalence and injection timing. | [Sun & Xia, “MS-based data preprocessing” and “Detecting outliers”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10827599/) motivates technical QC but does **not** certify the batch's 5:1, 50%, or 30% thresholds. The 9 blanks include pre-, middle- and post-run injections ([metadata](../../laure-weisskopf-group/docs/mapp_project_00016/mapp_batch_00280/metadata/treated/mapp_batch_00280_metadata.tsv)); pooled medians can conceal time-local carryover, and zero blank medians create `Inf` ratios. **Measured on the old published retained matrix:** of 4,167 features, 2,298 have fewer than three observed-positive signals across 40 pooled QCs; 1,869 have at least three, 1,868 at least 32, and 1,599 all 40. [`preprocess.R`](../src/biostats/preprocess.R) imputes QC nondetections before RSD, so a feature absent from **every** QC becomes constant and gets RSD 0, incorrectly passing as stable. Require observed-QC support *before* evaluating RSD, and report the observed count: that is a correctness change to what the CV means, not merely a preference for a new 30% cutoff. Calibrate any minimum observed-count threshold and inspect group-specific detection; pooled QC dilution can make a real condition-specific feature undetected, so **do not presume all 2,298 features biologically invalid**. Preserve numerical blank/RSD cutoffs pending raw-signal diagnostics and sensitivity, rather than calling passing features identified metabolites or proof of low technical error. |
| **3 · Conditional no-change: PQN and log2.** Retain as documented baseline, not a universal recommendation. | [Sun & Xia, “Probabilistic quotient normalization” and “Log transformation”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10827599/) explains PQN's *majority approximately unchanged* assumption and warns that log does not always normalize skew or remove outliers; [Du, pp. 6–7, 13–15](https://www.uab.edu/proteomics/metabolomics/workshop/2014/statistical%20analysis.pdf) distinguishes sample-wise from feature-wise operations and notes log cannot accept zero. Here PQN operates **after** heavy, global-minimum imputation and derives its reference from biological samples (or QC-only for the QC RSD), not an external invariant standard. Compare sample quotient factors, detection fractions, total signal, batch/timepoint and control trajectories; assess whether most features plausibly stay stable and whether PQN attenuates a real global treatment shift. Interpret contrasts on the log2 scale as geometric-ratio comparisons, not arithmetic-mean or absolute-concentration changes. |
| **4 · Established exploratory method; scaling is optional sensitivity.** Retain Pareto PCA alongside already-available unscaled/log-transformed views if useful; do not pick a scaling by the sharpest group separation. | [Sun & Xia, “Data centering and scaling”, “Pareto scaling”, “Unit variance scaling”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10827599/); [Du, pp. 9–14, 30–38](https://www.uab.edu/proteomics/metabolomics/workshop/2014/statistical%20analysis.pdf). Pareto divides centered features by √SD, between no variance scaling and autoscaling, but can still upweight noisy low-intensity features; QC/blank and missingness diagnostics should precede PCA interpretation. The study's Bray PCoA uses normalized, **nonnegative** values, not centered Pareto values. PCA/PCoA separation alone is not treatment evidence. |
| **5 · Established design requirement; do not erase confounding with a correction algorithm.** Keep within-batch/timepoint contrasts and matched-plant blocks; report which comparisons are identifiable. | [Du, pp. 23 and 26](https://www.uab.edu/proteomics/metabolomics/workshop/2014/statistical%20analysis.pdf) distinguishes independent two-sample from within-individual paired tests. Metadata show **age 6 only in B1 (81 samples); age 5 in B1/B2/B3 (36/107/121)**, with uneven treatment-by-age overlap: age and batch cannot be globally disentangled by adding both as unrestricted factors. Of 118 plant IDs, 112 have all T0/T1/T3, three have two timepoints, three one; [`paired_S49_D.yaml`](../../laure-weisskopf-group/docs/mapp_project_00016/mapp_batch_00280/recipes/paired_S49_D.yaml) blocks by `ATTRIBUTE_plant_uid`; [`model_cohort_T0.yaml`](../../laure-weisskopf-group/docs/mapp_project_00016/mapp_batch_00280/recipes/model_cohort_T0.yaml) adjusts batch, and [`variance_T0.yaml`](../../laure-weisskopf-group/docs/mapp_project_00016/mapp_batch_00280/recipes/variance_T0.yaml) stratifies permutations by batch. Respect within-plant correlation in longitudinal inference; a global PCA is descriptive and each row is **not** an independent plant. Report overlap/rank and age-by-treatment-by-batch tables before interpreting age, cohort, interaction or batch effects. Batch adjustment cannot identify an age effect absent age overlap. |
| **6 · Optional, *not yet justified*: injection-order drift correction, supervised discrimination, advanced imputation.** | The 40 QCs span orders **4–391**, maximum QC gap 13 injections and biological nearest-QC distance ≤6 (metadata audit). Of 1,868 retained features with ≥25 detected QCs, 662 have absolute Spearman correlation ≥0.5 between QC intensity and injection order (batch-input diagnostic): this is evidence to inspect trajectories, **not** proof that correction is valid or that instrumental drift rather than run/batch structure caused the trend. Inspect per-feature QC trajectories and independent biological controls, confirm QC material/protocol and smoothness, and only then compare a prespecified QC-based correction against unchanged data; beware of removing biological effects correlated with order/age/batch. Sun & Xia [“Dealing with zero values and outliers”, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10827599/) presents RF/KNN/QRILC as mechanism-dependent alternatives, not default winners on these LC–MS plant data. [Du, pp. 39–41](https://www.uab.edu/proteomics/metabolomics/workshop/2014/statistical%20analysis.pdf) explicitly cautions that PLS-DA can severely overfit; the study uses PCA/PCoA and a paired comparison explicitly disables PLS-DA. No opportunistic classifier, VIP biomarker claim, forced quantile normalization, or automatic drift correction on these citations alone. |

**Decision applied:** The observed-positive QC-support defect and global-minimum misimplementation warranted reusable, testable toolbox changes. Retain the batch's existing threshold choices as declared choices, but disclose feature attrition and inspect the new `feature_detection.tsv`, `blank_filter.tsv`, `qc_rsd.tsv` and `qc_drift.tsv` before interpreting discoveries. Correction for injection-order drift remains disabled for this batch; selecting it requires a scientifically prespecified design/trajectory check and a sensitivity comparison against the unchanged data. These sources do **not** establish a universal biological prevalence cutoff, an optimal missing-data mechanism, or a universally superior classifier.
